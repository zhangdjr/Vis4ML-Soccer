# Slice 01: Explainable ML for soccer prediction models, and whether explanation quality has been evaluated

Compiled 2026-09-27. Scope: xG, pass success, action value (VAEP/xT/EPV), tactical/tracking models, plus a few match-level models where they matter for the "explanation stability" question. Goal: **falsify** candidate gaps.

Labels: **FACT** = verified in a primary source I read (full text or abstract, as marked). **AUTHOR CLAIM** = what the authors assert, not independently checked. **SYNTHESIS** = my inference across sources. **SPECULATION** = unverified suggestion.

Evidence levels: FULL TEXT (I downloaded the PDF and read or grepped the relevant sections), ABSTRACT (only the abstract, from arXiv, Europe PMC, OpenAlex or Crossref), SECONDHAND (a search snippet, a figure caption, another paper's description, or a fetch-tool summary of the page).

Method note: I used WebSearch (budget ran out near the end), WebFetch, the arXiv API, the Semantic Scholar graph API (heavily rate-limited, so some calls failed), OpenAlex, Crossref and Europe PMC. Local PDFs are in `lit/pdfs/`.

## 1. PassAI: exact record (the anchor paper)

There are **two versions with different content**. This matters: the arXiv version has no faithfulness evaluation, but the journal version adds one.

| Authors | Ryota Takamido, Jun Ota (Univ. of Tokyo, RACE), Hiroki Nakamoto (NIFS Kanoya) | same |
| Where | arXiv:2503.08945 [cs.HC], 11 Mar 2025 (only v1 exists, FACT via arXiv API) | IEEE Access, vol. 13, pp. 132884-132898, 2025. DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2025.3589903. Gold open access, CC BY (FACT, Semantic Scholar and IEEE PDF) |
| Status | preprint | peer-reviewed journal (IEEE Access is a mega-journal; SYNTHESIS: the review bar is moderate) |

**Task and data (FACT, full text).** Binary pass success/failure for passes arriving within 30 m of goal. Data: 95 J1 League games (2023-2024) from Data Stadium Inc., 25 Hz tracking plus events. Passer season stats (15-d vector) scraped from jleague.jp. 6,349 passes (3,663 success, 2,686 fail). **The data is not public** ("not publicly shared owing to rights limitations"). No code link anywhere in either version (grep found no GitHub or code statement).

**Model (FACT).** Dual stream. (a) The tracking frame at pass time is rendered as a 224x224 RGB image: team colours, velocity arrows, and a dotted line from pass origin to where the ball arrived. This goes into an ImageNet-pretrained ConvNeXt-Tiny, giving a 768-d vector. (b) The stats vector goes into an MLP with one 64-unit hidden layer. The two are concatenated, then passed to an FC layer and softmax. Evaluation is 10-fold CV with an 8:1:1 split and flip augmentation. Baselines: GoogLeNet, ResNet50, EfficientNetV2, ViT, a pure-image ConvNeXt, a GCN, and CI-GNN. The journal version also adds SoccerMap and XGBoost as modified by Robberechts et al. (2023). Accuracy is 77.6%. AUTHOR CLAIM: ">5%" better than the prior state of the art. Also AUTHOR CLAIM: SoccerMap and XGBoost have *higher recall* than PassAI, and the event-based definition of the pass target may inflate all models' performance "by a few percentage points".

**Explanations (FACT).** Two stages. Stage 1 compares modalities using gradient sums, C_I = sum of dy/dx over image pixels and C_S = sum of dy/dv over stats features. These are min-max normalised across passes, so they can only be compared *between passes*, not between the two modalities. Stage 2 applies Grad-CAM on ConvNeXt's last conv layer for the image, and raw input gradients per stats feature.

**Explanation evaluation.**
- arXiv v1: **none**. They show qualitative figures only. AUTHOR CLAIM: some maps are "difficult to interpret" (Fig. 8). Future work says "the development of quantitative and concrete evaluation methods for generated explanations is essential (Lopes et al., 2022)".
- IEEE Access version: **faithfulness via ROAR (RemOve And Retrain, Hooker et al.)**. This is the main falsifier for "nobody evaluated soccer explanation faithfulness" (FACT). For the image branch, they mask the top 1/5/10% of Grad-CAM pixels and retrain a pure ConvNeXt. For the stats branch, they zero the top 1/5/10% of features and retrain PassAI with the image input held fixed.
  - Result (AUTHOR CLAIM): removing 1% of pixels causes a "substantial decrease" in accuracy. Accuracy then *recovers* at 5% and 10% removal, which is non-monotonic. The authors suggest noisy peripheral pixels or the model re-focusing.
  - Removing stats features does **not** change accuracy. The authors blame multicollinearity and the image stream compensating.
  - My critique (SYNTHESIS): I found no mention of a **random-removal baseline** in the text I could extract. ROAR is only interpretable when compared against one, but the tables are images, so this is UNVERIFIED. They also test only one attribution method (no comparison with IG, SHAP or occlusion), one model, and no stability. A null ROAR result on correlated tabular features is a known ROAR weakness, not proof that the gradients are unfaithful.
- Stated limitations and future work (AUTHOR CLAIM): the explanation methods "require further refinement, especially for the stats data"; they want sport-specific explanation techniques built with expert feedback; ConvNeXt and the MLP are off the shelf; they only used professional data. They also suggest image captioning or LLMs for explanations, calibrated probability maps, and a "what-if" tool that moves the arrival point or player positions.

**Papers citing PassAI.** Semantic Scholar lists 4, OpenAlex counts 6 (FACT, retrieved 2026-09-27). I do not know what the other 2 are.
1. Bajracharya & Husari, "Comparing Logistic Regression, Elo Ratings, and Hybrid Models for Predicting Sports Tournament Outcomes", IEEE AIIoT 2026. Match-outcome prediction, no XAI evaluation (ABSTRACT).
2. Giovannoni, Metta, Monreale, Rinzivillo, "A survey on multimodal explainable Artificial Intelligence", *Intelligent Systems with Applications* 2026. General multimodal XAI survey. Not read; abstract unavailable.
3. Afshar & Rahaman, "Pass Receiver Prediction in Football: Graph Neural Networks with Explainability Analysis", ABC 2026 (IEEE). A GAT on the MLSA18 pass dataset (12,124 passes, 14 games) with GNNExplainer edge importance. The abstract shows visualizations only; **no explanation evaluation is mentioned** (ABSTRACT).
4. Bayarsaikhan et al., "Dual-Attention Spatio-Temporal GNN for Dynamic Tactical Pattern Recognition in Football Matches" (TacGraphNet), ICMCSI 2026. Reports 96.8% accuracy (SYNTHESIS: implausibly high, and vague about the data). No XAI evaluation (ABSTRACT).

**None of the citing papers reproduces or extends PassAI's explanation evaluation** (SYNTHESIS, based on abstracts).

## 3. Deep notes on the most important papers

### 3.1 PassAI (#1): the closest prior work, and a reproduction target
- FACT: it is the only soccer paper I found that runs a **removal-based faithfulness test** (ROAR) on its explanations. That falsifies "nobody measured faithfulness in soccer XAI".
- FACT: the evaluation is thin. It uses one attribution method per modality, one model, three removal fractions, no reported random baseline (UNVERIFIED because the tables are images), and no stability or perturbation analysis. The stats-branch ROAR is a null result.
- FACT: the authors themselves list "quantitative and concrete evaluation methods for generated explanations" (arXiv v1) and "further refinement, especially for the stats data" (journal) as open problems.
- Reproducibility risk (FACT): the data is proprietary J1 and there is no code. SYNTHESIS: a faithful reproduction is impossible, so a "reproduction" would have to re-implement the architecture on public tracking or freeze-frame data. The image rendering is simple: positions, velocity arrows, and the origin-to-arrival line. SPECULATION, which the data-slice agent should verify: candidate public sources are StatsBomb 360 freeze frames, SkillCorner open data, the Metrica sample games, and PFF FC World Cup 2022 tracking.
- SYNTHESIS: the rendered input contains the **arrival point**, which is post-hoc information. It is analogous to the leakage Peters et al. (#14) found in xPT, and it probably explains why Grad-CAM focuses near the arrival point. A leakage-aware reproduction would itself be a defensible finding.

### 3.2 Tsai et al. 2026 (#2): the closest falsifier for "stability and agreement"
- FACT: it quantifies seed-wise stability, RF vs MLP agreement, and SHAP vs CIS agreement with Spearman rank correlation.
- FACT: it covers only global importance on a **match-level regression from 17 event counts**. The authors list local, context-specific explanations and spatio-temporal data as future work.
- SYNTHESIS: this falsifies "no one has measured stability or agreement of soccer explanations" *in general*. It does **not** cover local, per-action explanations of xG, pass or action-value models, tracking-based deep models, or input-perturbation stability. It is a preprint (May 2026) with 0 citations found.

### 3.3 Cefis & Carpita 2024 (#3): possible falsifier for "cross-model xG explanation agreement"
- FACT (abstract): 8 classifiers are compared on accuracy and on "explainability ... assessed using some appropriate metrics". The top features are consistent across classifiers.
- UNVERIFIED: which metric they used, whether the comparison was local or global, and whether it was quantitative. The full text is paywalled. **Any proposal claiming "first cross-model comparison of xG explanations" must first read this paper.**

### 3.4 Rahimian et al. 2025 (#4): evaluates *text* explanations
- FACT: it has a quantitative "accuracy" check. An LLM reads the generated text and states each feature's sign, which is compared with the true LR contribution. This evaluates whether the LLM layer is faithful to a *transparent* model. It does not evaluate post-hoc attribution faithfulness of a black box.
- FACT: human evaluation by coaches is explicitly deferred to future work.

### 3.5 Cavus / Biecek line (#6, #7, #22, #23)
- FACT: the soccer papers (#6, #7) display explanations with no evaluation. The group's robustness-flavoured work (balancing changes explanations; Rashomon multiplicity) uses **non-soccer data**.
- SYNTHESIS: nobody has joined these two lines. The "Rashomon set of xG models, and do their explanations disagree?" question appears open, and this group has public xG code and data (Understat) that could be reused.

## 5. Candidate gaps: falsified vs open

### FALSIFIED (someone did it)
1. **"No soccer paper tests explanation faithfulness."** Falsified by PassAI (IEEE Access 2025), which runs ROAR on Grad-CAM and stats gradients (FACT). *Remaining gap:* it tests one method with no reported random baseline, so the test is weak.
2. **"No one has measured stability or cross-model/cross-method agreement of soccer explanations."** Falsified *at the global, match level* by Tsai et al. 2026 (arXiv 2605.10796), using seed stability, RF vs MLP and SHAP vs CIS (FACT).
3. **"No one compared xG explanations across model families."** At least partly falsified. Cefis & Carpita 2024 (*Statistics*) compare 8 classifiers (the metric is UNVERIFIED), and Mead 2023 and Fu 2024 compare informally.
4. **"Nobody has used counterfactuals in xG/soccer."** Falsified in the loose sense. Mahmudlu et al. 2025 do "what-if" player swaps (not XAI), Vaykole 2025 uses DiCE counterfactuals in a thesis, and Tsai 2026 uses CIS. None of them *evaluates* the counterfactuals.
5. **"Nobody has noticed the evaluation gap."** Also false, but in a useful way. PassAI's authors and Kranzinger 2025 both explicitly call for quantitative or comparative evaluation of XAI in sports, which gives a proposal ready-made citations for its motivation.

### APPEAR OPEN (no paper found; these are the queries that returned nothing relevant)
A. **Local attribution disagreement across methods** (SHAP vs IG vs LIME vs Grad-CAM/occlusion) on a *per-shot or per-pass* soccer model.
B. **Rashomon or cross-model disagreement of local explanations** for near-equally accurate xG or pass models. The Cavus/Biecek Rashomon work is non-soccer.
C. **Input-perturbation stability** of explanations for tracking-based models, meaning explanation change under realistic tracking noise, frame offset, or player jitter. Zero papers found.
D. **Faithfulness of GNN explanations in soccer** (GNNExplainer or attention). Afshar 2026 and HIGFormer 2025 only display them.
E. **A stronger reproduction of PassAI's ROAR**: random baseline, deletion/insertion curves, handling of correlated stats, several attribution methods, and a leakage-free input (without the arrival point).
F. **Human evaluation** of soccer explanations with coaches or analysts.
G. **A visual-analytics tool for explanation disagreement/uncertainty in soccer models.** No VIS/TVCG/EuroVis hit.

### SYNTHESIS: the most defensible open question for a 40-hour project
Local explanations of soccer action models (xG, and PassAI-style pass models) have been shown but never checked for agreement across attribution methods, agreement across equally accurate models, or stability under realistic tracking noise. The only faithfulness test (PassAI's ROAR) covers one method, reports no random baseline, and gives a null result on the tabular branch.

A project could re-implement a PassAI-style model on public data as the reproduction, then add a multi-method, multi-model, perturbation-stability evaluation with a visual comparison interface as the extension. That would fill gaps A-C and E without colliding with Tsai 2026 (global, match-level) or Cefis & Carpita 2024 (global, cross-model xG). The proposal should still cite and differentiate from both.
