# Slice 06: Sports-analytics methodology, Claudio Silva's sports/XAI visual analytics, and human evaluation in soccer VA

Compiled 2026-09-27 for a solo NYU MS project (DS-GA 3001 Visualization for ML, Silva). Scope: public data only, about 40 hours, strong ML, weak frontend.

**How to read the labels**
- **FACT**: I checked it directly in the source (full text, or the verbatim abstract for ABSTRACT rows).
- **AUTHOR CLAIM**: the paper says it, but I did not check it independently.
- **SYNTHESIS**: my own inference from combining sources.
- **SPECULATION**: untested idea.
- **Evidence level**: how much of each source I read.
  - FULL TEXT: I downloaded the PDF and read the relevant sections with pdftotext and grep. I did not read every page.
  - FULL TEXT (summarizer): a web page was summarized by a fetch tool. Treat it as slightly weaker.
  - ABSTRACT: official abstract only, from PubMed, Crossref, OpenAlex or arXiv.
  - SECONDHAND: taken from a search snippet or from another paper's description.

**Corrections to the brief** (FACT)
- "Baseball4D (TVCG 2014)" was published in the **IEEE VAST 2014 conference proceedings** (pp. 23-32), not TVCG.
- "StatCast Dashboard (TVCG 2016)" is **IEEE Computer Graphics & Applications 36(5), 2016**.
- The methodology paper has an extra author: **Arne Jaspers**. Full list: Davis, Bransen, Devos, Jaspers, Meert, Robberechts, Van Haaren, Van Roy. *Machine Learning* 113:6977-7010 (2024).
- "Tac-Anticipator", "SoccerVis" and a Silva soccer paper could **not be found** (see B). I list them as not found rather than guessing.

**Where methodology meets XAI** (explanations that depend on split, leakage or shift)
- FACT: A8 shows SHAP rankings shift when leaky features are removed.
- AUTHOR CLAIM: A9 shows explanations reorder under league or level shift.
- FACT: A1 shows verification and counterfactual probing expose behaviour that aggregate test metrics hide.
- SYNTHESIS: I found **no soccer paper that holds the feature set fixed and varies only the split scheme** (random shot or action rows vs grouped-by-match vs temporal-by-season), then measures how global and local explanations change. That gap is concrete and cheap to fill with public StatsBomb data plus socceraction or soccer_xg.
- SPECULATION, a risk to check first: for **xG**, shots are fairly independent given their features, so the random-vs-grouped difference may be small.
- SYNTHESIS, why VAEP is a better test case: VAEP labels are "goal within the next 10 actions" and features include the previous actions. Consecutive actions in one possession therefore share near-identical labels and overlapping features. An action-level random split is likely to create near-duplicate train/test rows. The effect should be larger and easier to show, but that is a hypothesis to measure.

### Transferable principles from Silva's work (for a soccer ML VA project)
1. **Check data quality before modelling.** FACT: Baseball4D was built partly to audit tracking accuracy, with explicit consistency filters. SYNTHESIS: put event-data quality in view in a soccer tool too. A1's Veritas case (corner-square shots converting at 72%) shows that event-data labelling artefacts leak into xG.
2. **Query and filter across many plays, not single-play replay.** FACT: this runs through Baseball4D's filter widgets, StatCast Dashboard's querying interface, and ggViz's query-by-sketch. SYNTHESIS: for ML, this is subgroup selection. Brush shots or actions (zone, body part, league, season, team strength) and compare model behaviour per subgroup.
3. **Evaluate calibration per subgroup, not in aggregate.** FACT: Calibrate provides learned reliability diagrams and brushed subgroups. FACT: A1 and A4 argue for reliability diagrams or ECE and multicalibration for player subgroups. SYNTHESIS: a subgroup-calibration view of xG across split regimes is a direct "reproduce and extend" of Calibrate on a sports task, which Calibrate's own motivation mentions (sports betting).
4. **Use a temporal split and check calibration in your own sports models.** FACT: Silva's group's CS:GO valuation trained on earlier dates, tested on later ones, and reported Brier plus calibration curves. This is the practice A1 recommends.
5. **Check explanation disagreement and trust.** FACT: SUBPLEX and Visagreement treat explanations as objects to validate, not ground truth. SYNTHESIS: "explanations of the same model family disagree across split schemes" is a natural extension of Visagreement's "across methods" to "across evaluation protocols".
6. **Use static, time-explicit summaries.** FACT: Baseball Timeline uses a Marey-graph encoding. SPECULATION: a possession timeline of VAEP values (x = time or action index, y = value, colour = action type) is a cheap, explainable static view.
7. **Evaluate with a handful of experts.** FACT: Silva's group routinely uses **4-8 participants** in interviews or think-alouds, with no controlled studies in the papers checked: Baseball Timeline 4, ggViz 4, Calibrate 4, Visagreement 4, PipelineProfiler 6, SUBPLEX 8. SYNTHESIS: a 4-6 person think-aloud is therefore accepted evaluation practice in this instructor's own work.
8. **Build Jupyter-native widgets.** FACT: Calibrate, SUBPLEX and PipelineProfiler ship as Jupyter widgets. SYNTHESIS: this suits a weak-frontend student better than a standalone D3 app. Caveat (FACT): the 2024 course syllabus lists JavaScript and D3 as expected background, and projects are "reproduce prior work or implement a proposed research idea" in groups of 2-3, with individual projects optional.
9. **Record provenance of analysis state** (SPECULATION, in Silva's provenance tradition). Log which split, seed and feature set produced each explanation view, so comparisons are reproducible.

### Lightweight evaluation alternatives for a solo student
1. **Computational evaluation of explanations**, the primary option (SYNTHESIS; builds on A1, A3, A8, A9, B10):
   - Hold the model fixed and vary the split: random-row vs grouped-by-match vs temporal-by-season vs leave-one-competition-out. StatsBomb open data covers several competitions.
   - Report the test-metric gap (Brier, log loss, ECE with reliability diagrams per A1).
   - Report explanation stability: Spearman or Kendall rank correlation of global SHAP importance, top-k Jaccard (A3 already uses Jaccard for rankings), and per-instance attribution distance for shared test items.
   - Report split-half and season-to-season reliability of the derived player ratings (A1 §5.5, A3).
   - All automatic. No participants needed.
2. **Behavioural sanity probes** instead of users (A1 verification idea, with a simpler implementation). Build synthetic "what-if" grids: vary one feature (match time, angle, distance) with the others fixed, and check for implausible jumps (A1's 27-minute VAEP spike). Veritas itself may be heavy; plain grid probing is feasible (SPECULATION about effort).
3. **4-6 expert think-alouds**, 30-45 min each, remote. This precedent comes from the instructor's own lab (B3, B6, B7, B8, B9, B10: 4-8 participants) and from soccer VA (C2: 2 experts; C6: 4). Recruit from NYU soccer-analytics clubs or online analyst communities. Use a fixed task list: "which split would you trust?", "which feature looks leaky?". Report qualitatively. (SYNTHESIS)
4. **Usage scenarios or case studies** on public data, the field norm per C1. Reproduce a known finding (e.g. A2's "recency doesn't help", or A3's xT vs VAEP robustness gap), then show a new one through the tool.
5. **Small proxy-task pilot** with classmates (SPECULATION): 6-10 people predict model outputs (forward simulation) from two explanation designs. Label it clearly as a pilot, since classmates are not analysts.
6. **Do not use Mechanical Turk.** Skip crowdsourcing (C13 used 218 participants), because the target users are experts and the project has 40 hours.

## Bottom line for the RQ (SYNTHESIS)
- **Most defensible, lowest-risk framing:** "How much do explanations of soccer action-value and xG models depend on the evaluation split, and can a visual-analytics view reveal leakage-driven explanations?"
- **Reproduce:** a public xG model and VAEP with socceraction or soccer_xg on StatsBomb open data (the soccer_xg repo is FACT). Also reproduce A1's split recommendations, A3's robustness numbers, or Calibrate's subgroup reliability view (B9).
- **Extend:** add split-regime comparison of calibration and SHAP (novel per A8 and A9), shown in a Jupyter-native, Calibrate- or Visagreement-style view.
- **Evaluate:** mainly computationally, plus 4-6 think-alouds.
- **Risk:**
  - For xG, the split effect may be negligible (SPECULATION). Test VAEP first, where overlapping 10-action labels make leakage more plausible.
  - Frontend scope must stay minimal. The syllabus expects D3, so confirm with the instructor that a notebook-based tool is acceptable.
