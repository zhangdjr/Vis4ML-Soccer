# Slice 03: How saturated are XAI-evaluation questions in general ML/XAI and VIS?

Compiled 2026-09-27. Scope: stability, cross-method agreement, cross-model (Rashomon) agreement, visual analytics (VA) for explanation evaluation, and domain-grounded evaluation. The goal is to judge whether a soccer version would only be "a known method applied to a new domain".

**Labels:** FACT = verified from the source (abstract or full text). AUTHOR CLAIM = the paper says it, but I did not check it. SYNTHESIS = my inference from several sources. SPECULATION = my guess.
**Evidence levels:** FULL TEXT = I read all or a substantial part of the paper. ABSTRACT = abstract, proceedings page, or project page only. SECONDHAND = search snippets, citing papers, or a paywalled page I could not open.

---

## 0. Headline finding: the instructor's own lab has published on this exact problem

- **FACT:** Claudio Silva, who teaches DS-GA 3001, co-authored several works on comparing and evaluating local feature-attribution explanations for tabular binary classification. All were done with L. G. Nonato (USP) and Capital One (B. Barr):
  - **GALE** (ICML 2022 TAG-ML workshop). It compares sets of local explanations using topology (Mapper).
  - **MOUNTAINEER** (IEEE TVCG, arXiv 2406.15613). This is a VA tool for comparing local explanations. Its case study 1 compares disagreement across LIME, KernelSHAP, Occlusion, IG, Grad×Input, Vanilla Gradient, and SmoothGrad on HELOC. Its case study 2 compares LIME explanations across six models (LR, SVM, RF, NN, AdaBoost, XGBoost) on folktables ACSEmployment. That second study is effectively a cross-model or Rashomon comparison.
  - **Visagreement** (IEEE TVCG 31(10):7862-7875, 2025). This is a VA tool built specifically for "the disagreement problem" on tabular binary classification. It proposes a "(dis)agreement space".
  - **Silva, Silva & Nonato** (AAAI 2024 AI4Ed workshop, PMLR 257). It reports that agreement among 9 attribution methods correlates strongly with model performance on education data.
  - **A visualization-driven decision support system for selecting feature attribution methods** (Elsevier *Information Systems*, 2025; also known as "Explainalytics"). It has 7 metrics, 5 coordinated views, and a 10-person user study.
  - **P. Silva's IJCAI 2025 Doctoral Consortium paper**, which summarises the programme above.
- **SYNTHESIS:** Two of the four candidate angles are already covered by the grader's group: (b) cross-method disagreement, and (d) VA to compare and evaluate attributions on tabular data. So "SHAP vs LIME vs IG disagreement, visualised interactively, on soccer" will read as applying Visagreement or MOUNTAINEER to a new dataset. That is low novelty, and Silva will recognise it immediately.
- **The upside (SYNTHESIS):** these tools are the natural baseline and possibly reusable code (github.com/priscylla/visagreement). Their papers also leave specific gaps open, which a soccer study with known physics could address (see Section 3):
  - **FACT (IJCAI-DC full text):** Visagreement case study 1 "found no correlation between the metrics and the level of agreement or disagreement". Those were proxy quality metrics, because the real datasets had no ground truth.
  - **FACT (MOUNTAINEER full text):** its future work lists multi-class support, regression support, and comparing more than 2 graphs at once. Its stated limitations are one-vs-one comparison only and scaling to only "a few dozen explanation results".

## 2. Saturation verdicts

### (a) Stability under perturbation: **SATURATED for generic perturbations; THIN for domain-semantic perturbations on real data with known invariances**
- **Evidence (FACT):**
  - Metrics: local Lipschitz (#16); RIS, RRS, ROS (#19); OpenXAI and Quantus implement these (#26, #27).
  - Fragility and attacks (#17, #18).
  - Off-manifold critiques (#18, #24) and the correlated-features debate (#20-23).
  - Sampling multiplicity in SHAP (#13, 2026).
  - Metric proliferation: 41 metric groups across 362 papers (#31).
- **SYNTHESIS:** "Add Gaussian noise to the features and measure attribution change" gives no novelty. #13 also shows that L2-type stability can hide top-k rank instability, so a naive stability plot can be misleading.
- **What a soccer study can legitimately add (SPECULATION, grounded in #24, #33, #45, #48):**
  - Perturbations with physical meaning and known expected effects:
    - Mirror the shot across the pitch's long axis. xG and attributions should be invariant or mirrored.
    - Move the shot 1 m along an iso-distance arc. Distance attribution should stay about constant while angle attribution changes.
    - Change foot or body part while holding geometry fixed.
  - These are on-manifold by construction. Soccer supplies a known invariance or equivariance test that generic tabular benchmarks lack.

### (b) Cross-method agreement: **SATURATED**
- **Evidence (FACT):**
  - The canonical metrics and empirical study (#1).
  - A theoretical explanation of which methods should agree (#2).
  - Simulation and preprocessing confounds (#3).
  - Consensus training and aggregation (#4, #5).
  - **Two TVCG VA tools and one IJCAI-DC programme from the instructor's own lab (#35, #37, #38).**
- **SYNTHESIS:** A soccer version of "SHAP vs LIME vs IG disagree; here is a dashboard" is "known method applied to new domain". Worse, the instructor authored the known method.
- **What a soccer study can still add:**
  - Visagreement's case study 1 could not relate disagreement to correctness, because real datasets have no ground truth.
  - Soccer offers partial ground truth: monotone decrease with distance, and roughly monotone increase with goal-mouth angle, are physically motivated. So the study could test whether cross-method disagreement predicts deviation from a physics-derived reference.
  - It could also test whether finding (2) ("disagreement concentrates where the model is wrong") holds for xG. #32 already contests the general claim, so this is a live question.
  - SYNTHESIS: this is a narrow replication plus extension, but a defensible one.

### (c) Cross-model / Rashomon agreement: **ACTIVE (theory mature; empirical and VA work still moving, 2023-2026)**
- **Evidence (FACT):**
  - Theory and estimators: MCR (#6), VIC (#7), Partial Order (#8), RID (#9), and the position paper (#10).
  - Empirical studies: #11, #12, #13, #14 (weak), and #15, several from 2026.
  - VA: TimberTrek (#40, trees rather than attributions), GAM set (#41), VAR (#42, performance only), AI-Spectra (#43, predictions only), and MOUNTAINEER case study 2 (#35, LIME across 6 models, one-vs-one only).
- **SYNTHESIS:** Interactive visualisation of *attribution distributions and partial orders across many near-equivalent models* on tabular data looks **thin**. MOUNTAINEER limits itself to 1-vs-1 comparison and names "compare multiple graphs at once" as future work.
- **What a soccer study can add:** a small Rashomon set of xG models (for example GBM seeds and hyperparameters, a GAM, and logistic regression, all within ε log-loss). Show Laberge-style partial orders or RID-style intervals per feature. Check whether all good models agree on the physics-backed features (distance, angle) and disagree on contextual ones (for example defender counts or freeze-frame features). This is the angle with the least overlap with the instructor's lab. Caveat (SPECULATION): it needs careful control of seed variance (#11, #13).

### (d) VA to expose unfaithful-but-plausible explanations: **ACTIVE for generic comparison; THIN for ground-truth-anchored "plausible-but-wrong" detection**
- **Evidence (FACT):**
  - Comparison and selection tools: ExplainExplore (#39), GALE and MOUNTAINEER (#34, #35), Visagreement (#37), Explainalytics (#38).
  - The survey (#44).
  - The motivating failure modes: fooling (#18), suppressors (#48), and fairwashing detection (#15).
  - I found **no** VA system whose core task is "show where an explanation is plausible to a domain expert but contradicted by a faithfulness test or known ground truth". Caveat: that comes from a limited search, so treat it as a gap only after the VIS-slice check.
  - I also found no dedicated VIS paper on *uncertainty visualisation of attributions*. Only ML-side uncertainty-of-explanation methods came up. Same caveat.
- **What a soccer study can add:**
  - Soccer is a rare domain where the viewer, the course staff, and domain experts hold strong physical priors. That makes "plausible" operational.
  - Pair a plausibility check (agreement with physics priors, per #33's WoE idea) with a faithfulness check (OpenXAI PGI/PGU deletion tests, #26), and visualise the 2×2: plausible and faithful, plausible but unfaithful, and so on.
  - A seeded unfaithful model (Slack-style scaffold, #18, or a leaked feature) gives a known positive control. SPECULATION: this is the strongest single VA contribution possible in about 40 hours.

### Domain-grounded evaluation: **ACTIVE in geoscience and medicine; not found in sports**
- **Evidence (FACT):**
  - Synthetic domain-shaped ground truth (#45).
  - Metric-based domain evaluation without physical ground truth (#46).
  - Known dynamical system as testbed (#47, Aug 2026).
  - Expert-annotation ground truth in medicine (#49).
  - Calls for formal, use-case-specific correctness (#48).
  - Domain-hypothesis alignment (#33, Aug/Sep 2026).
- **SYNTHESIS:** "Use known physics to supply partial ground truth for evaluating attributions on a real (not synthetic) tabular task" is being pursued now in climate science. I did not find it for sports. It is the most defensible novelty hook.

## 3. Recommended positioning for the soccer project (SYNTHESIS)

1. **Do not pitch** "measure disagreement between SHAP, LIME, and IG and visualise it". That is Visagreement or MOUNTAINEER, from the grader's own lab.
2. **Do pitch:** *physics-anchored evaluation*. Use the known partial ground truth from soccer geometry in two ways:
   - Semantic perturbation tests: invariance, equivariance, and monotonicity.
   - A reference ordering: distance and angle should dominate for all good models.
   Then ask whether (i) cross-method disagreement, (ii) cross-model (Rashomon) disagreement, and (iii) standard faithfulness metrics (PGI/PGU) actually flag the explanations that violate physics.
3. **Explicitly cite and build on** Visagreement (#37) and MOUNTAINEER (#35). Frame the work as testing their open questions (disagreement vs correctness; multi-model comparison) in a domain with ground truth. This turns the overlap into a strength.
4. **Include a positive control:** a deliberately corrupted xG model (leaked feature or scaffold), so the VA's ability to expose plausible-but-unfaithful explanations can be demonstrated, not just claimed.
5. **Scope check (SPECULATION):** within about 40 hours, use TreeSHAP, KernelSHAP, LIME, and Captum IG on an MLP, plus OpenXAI metrics. Build a Rashomon set of about 20-50 GBM and GAM variants. Skip RID and full Rashomon enumeration.
